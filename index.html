UNDERSTANDING THE ATLAS

A relative index,
with a clear method.

Completed 5 October 2026 Primary_70_PA results: 7,706 clipped grid cells and 134 ward units. Each scale is fitted and normalized separately.

How HVI is built

Standardized and direction-harmonized indicators are weighted through PCA within each domain. Retention combines eigenvalue >1, individual variance ≥10%, and a 70% cumulative-variance target. A second centered correlation PCA uses 95th-percentile parallel analysis with 5,000 simulations to derive domain weights.

HVI raw = wE × Exposure + wS × Sensitivity − wA × Adaptive capacity

The final score is normalized to 0–1. Very Low ≤0.2; Low >0.2–0.4; Moderate >0.4–0.6; High >0.6–0.8; Very High >0.8. Higher adaptive capacity represents greater protection.

Read maps in context

Indicator maps use within-scale quintiles; their colors are not HVI classes. Ward and grid scores are relative to their own scale. Raster indicators are area means with mean imputation where needed. Small boundary fragments can be less representative. Supplied source values are preserved; the reanalysis did not independently reconstruct population density or indicator units.

Healthcare distances

Nearby care uses great-circle distance from the clicked location to recorded facility points. The HVI hospital-distance indicator is a separate raster-derived area mean and can differ. Current opening status, capacity and service availability are unverified.

This atlas describes relative vulnerability. It is not a current heat forecast or a prediction of illness.
